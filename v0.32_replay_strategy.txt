Best strategy: create a new branch from v0.32 and replay only your non-merge commits with cherry-pick.

Why this is the right approach:
- Your current branch contains upstream Metall commits after v0.32, including release v0.33 work by others.
- You want only your changes on top of v0.32, not the upstream history that came in through merges.
- git cherry-pick lets you apply just your selected commits and skip merge commits entirely.

Recommended workflow:

1. Identify your non-merge commits after v0.32.
2. Start a fresh branch from v0.32.
3. Cherry-pick your commits in chronological order.
4. Resolve conflicts as they come, because some of your commits were originally made on top of newer upstream code.
5. Run build/tests after the replay.

git checkout -b feature/privateer_deps_v032 v0.32
git cherry-pick <oldest-your-commit> <next> <next> ...

Why not rebase --onto?
- git rebase --onto v0.32 <base> current-branch is possible, but your branch contains merge commits and upstream history by others.
- Rebase in this case is more fragile and harder to reason about.
- Cherry-picking a curated list is more controlled and auditable.

Why not merge v0.32 into your current branch?
- That would not remove the upstream v0.33 commits already present.
- It would keep the mixed history you are trying to avoid.
